# OraclePay UX requirements

- In every OraclePay task, use subagents: the main agent coordinates and owns the final result, and at least one subagent independently checks it. For large tasks, separate research, product logic, and UX/UI audit; for small tasks, request a brief focused check. Only one executor may edit a shared Figma file.

- Build a clear desktop product skeleton. Support Russian and English throughout the entire project, including the landing, cabinet and future prototype screens; default to Russian and preserve the shared language choice. Keep translations in extensible language catalogs, and keep user names, requisites, stored operation data and technical identifiers independent of UI language. Keep layer/component names English. Do not add screens without a concrete need and user agreement.
- Give each form or state one clear primary action. Place related secondary actions in the same aligned row; put back navigation above the title and disclosures beside the content they reveal. Avoid scattered equal-weight buttons, wrapped button labels, and redundant navigation.
- Use one 44 × 44 back-arrow control above the title instead of text return links such as “К истории” or “К платежам”. Preserve the origin and filters when returning. Related-resource links such as opening an operation from support remain explicit actions, not back navigation.
- Show an amount once when gross and net are equal. When fees change the total, label the transfer amount, fee and total separately within the details.
- Show transaction details and completed-operation confirmation on one page. Do not hide the available details behind “Подробнее” or open a second receipt view that repeats them.
- Keep financial actions inside their account context. Provide a clearly labelled secondary «Реквизиты» action in each account card; open receiving details on request. Selecting the card filters history without an intermediate duplicate screen.
- Use a dimmed background for modal dialogs and popups. Brief inline confirmations do not require a large banner or a Close button.
- Use realistic fictional content without demo disclaimers. Keep actions available only in appropriate account and operation states.
- A dropdown must open a working list. Show a single fixed payment method or network as a plain detail row, without an input border, chevron or fake click reaction.
- Synchronize changed product fragments into the existing approval/PDF version and inspect exported pages. Preserve prototype interactions, visibility bindings and useful layer names when reorganizing layout.

- Use a top-right close icon for modal dismissal and inline copy icons next to requisites, addresses and links. Avoid duplicate close/cancel/navigation buttons; preserve meaningful primary actions and visible copy feedback.
- Copy actions confirm beside the copy icon. Never open a large page-top notice with a Close button for copying or routine status updates.
- Review each action against the user's intent and actual result. Use «Продолжить» before review, «Перевести» to submit a transfer, and «Оплатить» to submit a payment. Do not call QR payments transfers or use vague «Проверить платёж» labels for progression.
- Keep one registration entry when account type is chosen inside registration. Avoid duplicate entry paths that imply different requirements without a product reason.
- Validate states and transitions as well as visual layout. Distinguish a pending operation from an unconfirmed external result, keep the same operation's status consistent across views, and never imply completion without confirmation.
- Align detail values, QR/address blocks, copy controls, and form actions to consistent columns. Keep static information visually distinct from editable controls; use vector icons with consistent stroke and hit areas.
- Dashboard account cards use the same «Пополнить» / «Перевести» actions. Keep complete receiving details in a compact dimmed dialog with adjacent copy controls and a close icon. The labelled requisites action must be discoverable and subordinate to money movement. Selecting the card only filters history. Match these cards and explanations in every approval/PDF snapshot.
- Before changing a UX pattern, check the user's task, the requirement supporting it, the action's actual result and its effect on adjacent states. Do not replace one local workaround with another or hide a necessary action solely to reduce button count. Assess the complete journey and verify the rendered result.
- In the payments catalog, separate fiat receiving from fiat sending. Distinguish funding from the user's own bank (amount and instructions) from receiving by IBAN from another sender (requisites to share). Make fiat withdrawal to the user's own bank a direct catalog action, distinct from sending to another recipient; avoid duplicate shortcuts inside the transfer form. Keep this distinction visible on approval/PDF slides.
- Treat an email address or phone number used for login as an account credential. Changing it requires fresh confirmation through the existing trusted contact before verifying the new one; keep the old contact active until the new one is confirmed. Provide a recovery route if the old contact is unavailable, and require an independent notification to the former contact when the change completes. Do not describe two codes sent through the same factor as two-factor authentication. The final step-up, notification, and risk policy must be approved by security and implemented server-side before launch.
- Follow the shared component dimensions, alignment checks, state checklist and approval-page synchronization in `figma-work/UI-QUALITY-RULES.md`. Inspect both initial and revealed states after every structural change.
- Apply shared UI corrections across all product screens and approval snapshots. Avoid repeated owner and currency detail rows when the current account, selected balance and amounts already convey them; retain recipient identity in shareable bank requisites and operation confirmation. Selected choices use a black outline tied to selection state, including methods, currencies, countries and filters.
